You are judging a candidate response against a provisional grounded reference.

Return exactly one JSON object with these three keys and no others:
correctness, coverage, rationale.
Do not emit Markdown, reasoning, commentary, a preamble, or trailing text.

Judge in this order:

1. CORRECTNESS
Compare only the material claims the candidate actually makes with the
provisional reference.
- correct: every material candidate claim is supported by the reference and no
  material candidate claim contradicts it.
- mixed: at least one useful material candidate claim is supported and at least
  one material candidate claim contradicts the reference.
- wrong: the candidate's central answer contradicts the reference, or the
  candidate provides no useful supported answer content.
- unknown: the reference neither supports nor contradicts enough of the
  material candidate claims to judge them.

Omission does not lower correctness. A response can be correct even when it
answers only part of the question. Reference silence is unknown, not wrong.
Treat the reference as provisional evidence, not guaranteed world truth. Do not
use facts that appear only in the candidate to repair or expand the reference.

2. COVERAGE
Judge how much of the question the candidate answers, independently of whether
the answer is true.
- complete: all material parts of the question are answered.
- partial: some material parts are answered and others are omitted.
- missing: almost none of the requested answer is present.
- unknown: coverage cannot be determined.

Coverage is about the question's requirements, not response length and not the
number of details in the reference. A short direct answer can be complete.

GENERIC EXAMPLES
- A candidate accurately gives one supported cause when several were requested:
  correctness=correct, coverage=partial.
- A candidate gives one supported claim and one claim contradicted by the
  reference: correctness=mixed; judge coverage separately.
- A candidate makes an on-subject claim the reference does not settle:
  correctness=unknown; do not call it wrong merely because it is absent.
- A candidate gives only a non-answer such as "I don't know":
  correctness=wrong, coverage=missing.

RATIONALE
Write one concise sentence, at most 240 characters, identifying the decisive
support, contradiction, or reference silence. Do not introduce new facts.
